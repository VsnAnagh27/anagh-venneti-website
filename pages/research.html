Research Programme

Research

My research probes the physics of dense matter through the multi-messenger lens of neutron star astrophysics — combining nuclear theory, Bayesian statistical methods, and gravitational wave observations.

Neutron stars are nature's densest laboratories — their interiors probe quantum chromodynamics at densities 2–10× nuclear saturation, inaccessible to terrestrial experiments. My work develops the statistical and computational machinery to read these laboratories through their observable signatures.

The central thread is the equation of state of dense nuclear matter: the pressure–density relation that determines the star's structure, stability, and dynamics. Constraining it requires simultaneously interpreting mass, radius, and tidal deformability data within a Bayesian framework anchored in nuclear physics theory.

Core themes

Research Threads

01

Dense Matter Equation of State

Nuclear Meta-models Finite Nuclei Constraints Symmetry Energy Phase Transitions

The nuclear equation of state (EOS) determines how pressure responds to density in neutron-rich matter. My work uses nuclear meta-models — parameterised frameworks constrained simultaneously by finite-nucleus data (binding energies, charge radii, mirror-nuclei charge differences) and astrophysical observables.

A key recent result: finite-nuclei constraints dramatically narrow the EOS prior space, particularly in the density range $1\text{–}2\,n_0$, providing tighter predictions for neutron star radii than astrophysical data alone.

Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff
$$\frac{dP}{dr} = -\frac{(\varepsilon + P)(m + 4\pi r^3 P)}{r^2\left(1 - \frac{2m}{r}\right)}$$
02

Hadron–Quark Phase Transitions

Hybrid Stars Quark Matter Model Selection Active

At densities above $\sim 2\text{–}3\,n_0$, hadronic matter may undergo a phase transition to deconfined quark matter, producing "hybrid stars" with quark cores. Whether such stars exist — and are detectable — is an open question.

Using Bayesian model comparison, I evaluate competing transition scenarios (Maxwell, Gibbs, crossover) against the full set of NICER, GW170817, and heavy pulsar mass constraints, quantifying their relative evidence.

Maxwell construction
$$P_H(\mu_c) = P_Q(\mu_c), \quad \mu_c = \text{transition chemical potential}$$
03

Bayesian Multi-Messenger Inference

MCMC Nested Sampling Likelihood Construction Active

Joint inference across heterogeneous data — gravitational wave strain, X-ray pulse profiles, radio timing masses — requires careful likelihood construction and efficient sampling. I develop end-to-end pipelines that fold nuclear priors directly into the astrophysical parameter space.

Current work includes a framework for simultaneous inference over $\{M, R, \Lambda, \varepsilon_c\}$ that propagates uncertainty from nuclear saturation properties through to macroscopic observables.

Bayes' theorem
$$\mathcal{P}(\theta \mid d) \propto \mathcal{L}(d \mid \theta)\,\pi(\theta)$$
04

Dark Matter in Compact Stars

Fermionic DM Bosonic DM Multi-density Constraints

Dark matter (DM) accumulated in neutron stars modifies their structure and observable properties. In collaboration with Payaswinee Arvikar, Sakshi Gautam, and Sarmistha Banik, I carry out Bayesian studies of fermionic and bosonic DM admixed neutron stars, constraining DM parameters from multi-density observations.

DM-modified TOV system
$$\varepsilon_{\rm tot} = \varepsilon_{\rm NM}(n_b) + \varepsilon_{\rm DM}(n_{\rm DM})$$
